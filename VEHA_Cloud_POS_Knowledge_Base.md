- VEHA is presented and marketed as a local-market POS offering for Cambodian businesses. [PROJECT] [SITE]

## 4. Target audience and use cases

### Core customer groups

1. **First-time coffee-shop owners:** Need reliable checkout, menu setup, team onboarding, a cash drawer routine, and a way to understand daily sales. [PROJECT]
2. **Small existing cafés:** May outgrow notebooks and want to reduce missed orders, inaccurate totals, and uncertainty over which drinks sell. [PROJECT]
3. **Restaurants and food outlets:** Need modifiers/add-ons, table-based service, split bills, order handling, and reports. [MANUAL] [SITE]
4. **Takeaway and counter-service outlets:** Need fast product selection, checkout, receipts, and organized shift handovers. [MANUAL] [SITE]
5. **Multi-staff shops:** Benefit from support for multiple user accounts and staff-related shift/report workflows. [PROJECT] [SITE]
6. **Small retail shops:** Also addressed by the public offering, although the team's strongest product-specific narrative is food-and-beverage. [SITE]
7. **Growing operators:** Potential users of upgraded hardware and optional additional ordering channels; confirm deployment-specific features before selling them. [SITE]

### Buyer concerns repeatedly addressed

- “A notebook costs nothing. Why pay for a POS?”
- “Can my staff learn it quickly?”
- “Will a POS help me understand sales versus actual profit?”
- “What if a cashier sells something that is out of stock?”
- “Can different employees use the same system?”
- “Do I need to replace my existing hardware?”
- “What happens at shift change and at the end of the day?”
- “Which package makes sense for my budget and counter layout?”

These are marketing/sales use cases, not independently validated performance claims. [PROJECT]

## 5. VEHA's core value propositions

### 5.1 Purpose-built service workflows

VEHA's messaging should lead with the activities cafés and restaurants perform each day: items, sizes, toppings, drinks, orders, discounts, bills, table handling, and payments—not a long technical feature inventory. [PROJECT] [MANUAL]

### 5.2 Better visibility than notebooks

A POS can provide consistent transaction records and reports, reduce arithmetic/transcription effort, make it easier to review transactions across shifts, and help owners see which products sell. **Do not claim a POS automatically calculates net profit or actual food cost without confirmed cost and expense data.** [MANUAL] [SITE] [PROJECT]

### 5.3 Practical prevention of unavailable-item orders

The team has specifically promoted the ability to mark unavailable products so a cashier does not sell an out-of-stock menu item. The manual documents `In stock`, `Out of stock today`, and `Delisted` statuses; the test sheet records the sale-status function as completed. Confirm exact on-screen disablement behavior against the production build. [PROJECT] [MANUAL] [TEST]

### 5.4 Multiple users without minimizing the package

The team described support for an **unlimited number of users**, correcting an earlier mistaken use of the word “seats.” The pricing page explicitly says “Multi-users,” and the trial page says “Multi-user support.” Treat exact lack of account/device limits as a claim to confirm for contractual wording. [PROJECT] [SITE]

### 5.5 Professional counter hardware

The team has argued that a full-size, fixed POS terminal is harder to casually pocket than a handheld mobile ordering device. This was used as a creative concept involving a theft-of-mobile-device video. Do **not** claim a fixed terminal cannot be stolen; frame it as a more stable, prominent, purpose-built checkout setup. [PROJECT]

### 5.6 Local relevance and simplicity

VEHA speaks to Cambodian café owners, creates English/Khmer content, promotes a Khmer-friendly onboarding experience, and offers local-market prices and product packages. [PROJECT] [SITE]

## 7. Detailed product administration from the user manual

Source: *VEHA Cloud POS User manual.pdf*, “Product” chapter. [MANUAL]

### 7.1 Back Office access and product navigation

- Log into the **Back Office**.
- Navigate to **Products → Product Management → Product List** to work with categories and products.
- The manual contains procedural screenshots for the setup flow, but this knowledge base describes the visible written instructions rather than reproducing the screenshots.

### 7.2 Categories

- Add a category and give it a name.
- Optionally upload an image.
- Edit category names and images.
- Set visibility to **Show** or **Hide**.
- Delete a category.
- Sort categories/products to control display order.

### 7.3 Individual items/products

An item form includes the following fields or options:

| Field / option | Meaning in manual |
| --- | --- |
| Name | Item name |
| Description | Optional explanatory description |
| Product code | Item identifier/code |
| Category | Category to which the item belongs |
| Picture | Product image |
| Button color | Color used for the product button |
| Specification | E.g., medium/large size variants and associated prices |
| Meal availability | `In stock`, `Out of stock today`, or `Delisted` |
| Default specification | Select default size/variant |
| Weighing product | Use a selling unit such as litre, kilogram, or pack |
| Open price item | Allows cashier to change the price during an order |
| Duty-free product | No tax applied to that item, per the manual's terminology |
| Tax | Manual describes a default tax setting of **10%** |
| Modifiers / add-ons | Associate customization options with the item |

**Tax caution:** “Default 10%” is a configuration described in the manual, **not tax advice or a statement that every transaction in Cambodia should always be taxed at 10%**. Actual applicability depends on local tax rules and merchant status. [MANUAL]

### 7.4 Editing, deleting, and sorting

- Items can be edited using the edit/pencil action.
- The editable fields largely mirror product creation.
- Products can be deleted after confirmation.
- Categories and products can be manually reordered.

### 7.5 Quick edit / bulk operations

1. **Bulk Price Change** — Select products and enter updated prices. The manual also mentions a “Bulk Change Open Price” action.
2. **Set Sale Status** — Bulk change item availability to `In stock`, `Out of stock today`, or `Delisted`.
3. **Batch Set Tax** — Select items and apply chosen tax settings.
4. **Import Products** — Download an Excel template; fill product names, codes, descriptions, specifications, prices and categories; upload the completed workbook.

### 7.6 Modifiers

- Navigate to **Products → Product Management → Modifier**.
- Create a modifier name with subordinate options.
- Link the modifier to specific products.
- Save changes.

**Example relevant to café content:** Coffee temperature or other product-specific modifications. This example is illustrative; exact predefined options depend on the merchant's configuration.

### 7.7 Add-ons

- Navigate to **Products → Product Management → Add-on**.
- Create an add-on and its sub-options.
- Define quantity limits (one or more selections).
- Optionally require at least one selection.
- Link add-ons to chosen products.

**Examples:** Extra espresso shot, alternative milk, syrup, or toppings. These are configuration examples, not a claim that VEHA supplies a built-in catalogue of ingredients.

## 8. Detailed sales, order, and shift workflows

Source: *VEHA Cloud POS User manual.pdf*, “Order” chapter. [MANUAL]

### 8.1 Log in and begin a shift

1. Log into the POS terminal.
2. Select **Start shift**.
3. Enter the **starting cash in the drawer**.
4. Confirm shift opening.

### 8.2 Standard sale/order flow

1. Open the menu/order function after beginning the shift.
2. Select the applicable **dining option**.
3. Tap a product to order it.
4. Select its required **modifier** or **add-on** choices.
5. Add the configured item to the cart.
6. Proceed to checkout and confirm the order.
7. Enter the actual cash received if collecting cash, and choose the relevant configured payment method.
8. Confirm checkout to close the order.

### 8.3 Order corrections and options

- Edit an ordered item's modifier, add-on or quantity.
- Remove a product from an order.
- Apply a discount; the manual describes fixed discounts, an amount discount, or a percentage.
- Split an order by moving selected items into a new bill.
- Navigate between the resulting bills.

### 8.4 Shift close and end of day

1. Open the user/shift interface.
2. View shift details, including the sales total.
3. Select **Ending Shift**.
4. Enter the **actual ending cash**.
5. Close the shift.
6. Print the **shift report**.

### 8.5 Operational functions found in test records

The test workbook additionally refers to the following POS operations: printer setup, delete-before-send, cancel-after-send, service charge, separate/move/merge order, change shift, cash and e-payment, and end of day. Their recorded completion status and limitations are addressed in Section 14. [TEST]

## 17. Marketing strategy and messaging themes

### A. The “notebook vs. POS” campaign

The team explored how to persuade coffee shops to upgrade from handwritten sales records. The strongest angle is not that notebooks are foolish or worthless: they are **simple, familiar and nearly free**. VEHA can instead highlight where notebooks become inconvenient as sales, menus, staff and customer volumes grow. [PROJECT]

Promising angles to test:

- Review a full day of sales without adding every receipt by hand.
- Make shift reconciliation more structured.
- Reduce order and pricing inconsistencies.
- Know which menu items sell most often.
- Support multiple people taking orders.
- Keep a menu organized when products, sizes, or prices change.
- Grow from owner-operated counter to a small team without changing the sales workflow completely.

### B. Built for food and beverage

The team repeatedly contrasted restaurant-oriented functionality with generic apps that are later fitted for café/restaurant use. The recommended proof is concrete: modifiers, sizes, table operations, split bills, order statuses, printers and shifts. [PROJECT] [MANUAL]
